# Methodology and assumptions

## Scope

This portfolio case demonstrates a monthly management model (July–September 2026), a bridge between accrual profit and cash movement, and a September cash-flow review for two anonymized legal entities.

All names are generic and the figures are illustrative/anonymized assessment data. The original source workbook and PDF are not included.

## Monthly model

- Revenue, cost of goods sold (COGS), operating expenses, depreciation, and interest are modeled on an accrual basis.
- EBITDA = revenue − COGS − operating expenses.
- EBIT = EBITDA − depreciation.
- Profit before tax = EBIT − interest expense.
- Income tax is modeled at 20% of profit before tax.
- Net profit = profit before tax − accrued income tax.
- Cash collections assume 75% of current-month revenue plus 25% of the prior month's revenue.
- Supplier payments assume 80% of current-month COGS plus 20% of the prior month's COGS.
- Operating cash flow = collections − supplier payments − operating expenses paid − tax paid.
- Net cash flow includes operating, investing (CAPEX), and financing flows (principal, interest, and dividends).
- Opening cash for July is an illustrative RUB 300,000.

These are simplified planning assumptions, not accounting or tax advice. Replace them with company-specific terms before operational use.

## September transaction review

- Internal transfers are excluded from consolidated inflows and outflows. They remain visible as reference items because only one side of the sample transfer is present.
- Customer refunds are shown as a separate operating cash outflow. In an accrual P&L, a sales refund may reduce revenue rather than be treated as an operating expense.
- Inventory purchases are operating cash flows in this example because they relate to working capital, not acquisition of a long-term asset.
- Entity-level cash flow is shown separately to expose cash distribution between legal entities.
- The opening cash balance is not provided for the September transaction sample, so closing cash is not calculated.

## Reconciliation logic

A simplified bridge from net profit to cash movement considers depreciation, changes in receivables and payables, tax timing differences, CAPEX, loan principal repayments, dividends, and other non-operating movements.

A bridge should reconcile to the change in cash. Any residual must be investigated rather than forced to zero.

## Limitations

The September transaction sample does not include opening bank balances, complete bank statements, tax detail, debt schedules, inventory balances, full receivables/payables ledgers, or the second side of the internal transfer. It supports a cash-flow classification exercise, not a complete liquidity diagnosis.
